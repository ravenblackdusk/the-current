# Living with the Current

*Practical companion*

What do I do with this? How does understanding the Current change my life?

It gives me a question to bring to my choices: how can I help what comes after me become more capable of persisting? The answer depends on my abilities, circumstances, and what others need. The aim is to maximize the chance of persistence; the ways of doing that can change. The examples below are situational guidance, while [The Current and the Purpose of Life](./) develops the foundational argument.

## Marriage

My present advice is to get married. I give weight to marriage as a tradition that, in my judgment, has worked well enough to be carried forward. A lasting partnership can provide a setting for raising children, sharing responsibilities, and supporting each other's contributions. That is a practical reason to consider it, rather than a claim that every person in every possible future must marry.

## Children

Having children is a direct way to carry something of yourself into the future. Raising them also lets you pass on knowledge, habits, and an understanding of the world. The contribution includes helping them become capable of making contributions of their own. Thinking about the Current helped give marriage and children a place in my own plans.

## Genetic contribution

Ask yourself whether you have inherited characteristics worth passing to the next generation. My advice is to pass on characteristics that benefit the Current and refrain from passing on those that hinder it. The standard is their actual effect on the capacity for persistence. People can disagree about that effect and be mistaken; disagreement does not give each person a separate truth.

I regard strength, intelligence, beauty, kindness, and height as generally favorable examples, and genetic disease, low intelligence, weakness, and unattractiveness as generally unfavorable ones. Beauty matters partly because it helps other traits spread: an intelligent person who is also attractive is more likely to find a partner and have children. Pairing beauty with intelligence and other useful traits helps carry those traits forward. These are claims about their effects, open to examination and correction, rather than declarations of personal taste. They are not a complete ranking or a genetic test: visible characteristics alone do not tell us exactly what a child will inherit. Traits must be considered together and in the conditions where they operate. A smaller overall positive contribution still counts.

## Discoveries

A discovery can expand what future generations are able to understand and do. Newton illustrates how a person's intellectual contribution can reach far beyond their lifetime without passing through children of their own. You do not need to equal Newton for your work to matter. Solving a smaller problem, finding a useful method, or adding reliable knowledge can give others a starting point they would otherwise lack.

## Teaching and preserving knowledge

You can contribute by making existing knowledge available and understandable. Teach someone a skill, document what you have learned, or preserve information that would otherwise disappear. A discovery has little continuing effect if nobody can learn from it. Helping knowledge pass between people and generations can be your part in the Current.

## Building and maintaining

Contribution also includes making useful things work. Building tools, improving a process, maintaining infrastructure, or repairing something others depend on can increase their ability to continue and create. The work need not be famous or unprecedented. Its significance lies in what it enables.

## Care and support

You can contribute through someone else's contribution. Caring for a dependent, supporting a partner's work, or helping another person develop their abilities can enable something you could not accomplish alone. Raising a child is an example: the child initially depends on others, while the contribution that care enables may come much later. Assess the support and what it enables across the relevant span of a life.

These paths can overlap. Non-genetic contribution can be a first choice, not merely a substitute for having children. Your task is to judge where you can contribute best, rather than to do everything on this list.

## Understanding the compass

Through the processes that produced us, the universe has also provided us with a compass. Hunger, the appeal of food, and sexual desire motivate actions connected to survival and reproduction before we consciously understand why those actions matter. This alignment is the Current at work in us: the same process that brought us into existence also shaped what we want.

Food illustrates both the usefulness and the limits of this compass. Eating supplies energy and nutrients we need to survive. But the attraction of calorie-rich food does not tell us exactly how much we need, or guarantee that what appeals to us is nutritious. When food is abundant, following that attraction can lead us to eat more than we need. Understanding nutrition and our circumstances can help us make a better decision than simply following the immediate desire.

Sexual desire provides another example. It can motivate reproduction without any conscious understanding of the Current. It was shaped long before contraception existed, so it cannot tell us whether a particular act will lead to children. Once we recognize the direction it has helped serve, we can consider when following its signal contributes to that direction and when a different choice would serve it better. This does not make every nonreproductive expression of sexuality contrary to persistence.

The compass is an estimate, not a rule. It was tuned to the conditions in which it evolved, and those conditions have changed faster than it can. Its existence shows the Current reaching into our motivations; its limitations explain why conscious understanding matters. We can use what we learn about the direction to improve on the guidance we inherited.

## A speculation about future motivation

Our inherited motivations will keep changing. If abundant calories shorten lives or reduce the number of children people raise, future generations may inherit a weaker attraction to energy-rich food. The pressure may be small, because much of the harm arrives after people have already had children. Similarly, if people increasingly choose to have children because they understand the Current, reproduction would depend less on sexual desire. Could that change the evolutionary pressures that maintain it? I do not know. These are predictions, not foundations.

## A personal aside on walking

I usually walk faster than the people around me. When foot traffic forced me to slow down, I used to become annoyed and impatient. Then, on one occasion, a thought occurred to me: we are all moving in the same direction. Some move slowly and others quickly. Sometimes the slower walkers hold up the faster ones, but we are still moving in the same direction.

That thought reduced my dislike for people I considered incompetent. I could see them as fellow participants, even when their pace frustrated me.
