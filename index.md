This essay develops the foundational claims of the philosophy. Personal applications are collected separately in [Living with the Current](living-with-the-current.html).

I believe the universe has a purpose, and that we can recognize something of its direction without knowing its final destination. That direction is toward greater capacity for persistence: the ability of patterns to continue through changing conditions and increasingly difficult challenges. I call it **the Current**.

We are products of this process. We are also capable of understanding it and deliberately contributing to it. Our place in the future depends in part on what we do with that ability.

## Persistence through change

A thing can remain for a long time because nothing has seriously threatened it. Another can survive because it can respond when circumstances change. These are different kinds of persistence. My concern is especially with the capacity to continue when simply remaining as one is would no longer be enough.

Persistence does not require every component to remain unchanged. An organism replaces material. A population continues through generations whose individual members die. Knowledge passes between people, sometimes surviving the loss of the society in which it originated. In each case, something continues through changes in what carries it.

I use the word *pattern* for this continuity of organization or information. A pattern can persist through reproduction, adaptation, transmission, or the survival of the system that embodies it. Deciding how much change a pattern can undergo before it becomes something else is a real question. But we already recognize continuity without requiring identical material or immortal individuals.

This also leads me to a broader use of *life* than the usual biological category. I understand life in terms of a pattern's capacity for persistence. The question becomes how capable something is of continuing, rather than only whether it belongs on one side of a living–nonliving boundary.

Under this view, a solar system that produces beings capable of protecting or carrying its patterns beyond their original home gains a possibility of persistence that it previously lacked. Its conscious inhabitants become part of its capacity to respond to threats.

## Time and the emergence of greater capacity

Time continually puts the universe to the test. Physical processes form and reshape galaxies, stars, and solar systems; some arrangements endure while others break apart. Within some of these systems, planets provide conditions in which life can arise. Life, in turn, can give rise to consciousness. I see these developments as stages of the same Current: through time, new patterns emerge and face the test of whether they can continue.

In this view, time forces the issue of persistence at every stage. What endures under one set of conditions may fail under the next. A pattern can continue only while it has, or develops, the capacity to meet the challenges it encounters. Across the many possibilities unfolding in the universe, I see this pressure driving the emergence of greater capacities: from enduring cosmic structures to life, from life to consciousness, and perhaps from consciousness to deliberate acceleration of the Current.

Most possibilities need not succeed. A universe can contain enormous numbers of failures while still producing rare developments of immense consequence. The rarity of life and consciousness, if they are rare, makes them especially valuable: they represent capacities that many other histories never reach.

Time matters because it allows further possibilities to arise and subjects them to further challenges. Reproduction can preserve a pattern beyond an individual's lifetime. Adaptation can preserve a lineage through environmental change. Understanding can make a threat actionable before it arrives. The capacity to improve one's own chances of persistence is itself a further development.

This progression does not require every solar system to produce life or every living lineage to become conscious. The direction is visible in what becomes possible through those that continue. The next step may be consciousness that recognizes the Current and deliberately helps the required capabilities develop before the next deadline.

## Information

One way to follow the Current is to follow information. A star or a planet carries little information about anything beyond itself. A self-replicating molecule stores instructions for making more of itself. An organism uses information about its surroundings to respond to them. Nervous systems, language, writing, and computers each increased how much information could be stored, copied, and acted on, and each arrived sooner after the last than its predecessor did. Earth and Mars formed from the same disk of gas and dust around the Sun, yet Earth now holds vastly more organized information than Mars.

Information matters to persistence because it lets a pattern prepare for what it has not yet encountered, and because it can move to new carriers: a genome to an offspring, knowledge to a student, data to another machine. Its concentration in some places seems to be increasing, and the increase seems to be accelerating. Quantity alone is not the measure; noise is information too. But asking whether a development increases how much useful information can be preserved, transmitted, and acted on gives the Current something closer to a measure than capacity for persistence alone.

## Uncertain success

I do not know the probability of ultimate success. Repeated opportunities give grounds for hope where success is possible, but we do not know how many opportunities there will be or what every future challenge will require. My intuition is that we can succeed for a while, perhaps far beyond our present expectations. Discovering how far requires us to try.

Physics as we currently understand it predicts that the universe will eventually run down. As usable energy is spent, the differences that structures depend on fade, and nothing organized may be able to continue. If that prediction holds, the Current has a limit. That would bear on whether the Current is eternal, not on whether it exists. Our knowledge of physics is incomplete, and we will never learn where the real limits lie if we do not try our best to reach them.

## Recognizing purpose without knowing the destination

Imagine alien robots appearing in many places on Earth. Their purpose is to reach the North Pole and contact their mothership, but the people observing them do not know this.

The robots perform apparently unrelated activities. Some cross land. Others reach a shore, build boats, or buy tickets. Some help other robots. At first, an observer might see only separate actions and local objectives.

Over time, a pattern emerges. Robots converge on particular ports. Their latitude generally increases. Different actions begin to make sense as different responses to the same underlying task. An observer could recognize a shared purpose before discovering the exact destination or the call to the mothership.

This is how I approach the universe. We need not know the ultimate destination to recognize a direction in the processes that produced us. I interpret the recurring emergence of greater capacities for persistence as evidence of purpose.

The analogy illustrates the inference; it does not prove it. My claim is that the recurring direction across those processes reveals something about what the universe is moving toward. Conscious intention need not have existed at the beginning for purpose to become recognizable in what unfolds.

Persistence is the direction we can presently identify. The purpose it leads to remains unknown. We can understand something about the next part of a journey without possessing a map of its end.

## Conscious participation

Consciousness makes it possible to recognize threats, compare possible responses, and act on events that have not yet happened. It allows parts of the universe to understand something of the processes that formed them and influence what follows.

We already know that some conditions on which we depend will not last forever. The Sun will eventually expand into a red giant, engulfing Mercury and Venus and possibly Earth. Even if Earth escapes being swallowed, its surface will no longer be habitable as the Sun ages. [NASA describes this future here.](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/resources/life-and-death/chapter-6/)

We do not yet know whether our descendants will meet this challenge successfully, or what form a successful response would take. Other extinction threats could arrive much sooner. A capacity developed after the event that required it is too late. Moving in the right direction therefore may not be enough: we may also need to move fast enough to meet the next deadline. Conscious participation in the Current means using foresight to improve our chances of doing so.

Consciousness is therefore not necessarily the destination of the Current. It is a means by which persistence can become more capable. Recognizing the Current may be another development: beings able to pursue particular goals can also ask whether those goals contribute to continuation beyond themselves.

The criterion is contribution to persistence. Personal attachment alone does not establish that preserving something advances the Current. Nor does the mere continuation of a particular individual settle what best serves the larger patterns in which that individual participates.

Why should anyone take part? Because there is no neutral option. Patterns that do not contribute to their own continuation eventually end, and their place in the future passes to those that do. Sooner or later, some beings in the universe will recognize the Current and deliberately accelerate it. That will give them an advantage others cannot match, and the future will belong to them. Each of us can be part of that future or a failed experiment it learns from.

There can be many ways to contribute to one direction. Recognizing a shared purpose does not mean that everyone must perform the same task.

## Choosing under uncertainty

The practical aim is to maximize the chance of persistence. Speed matters when it helps achieve that aim. Faster is not always better.

A development that promises new capabilities can also endanger capabilities we already depend on. Taking time to understand it may improve the chance of lasting progress. In other circumstances, delay may close an opportunity. The relevant question is what improves persistence, given the circumstances and what we can learn about them.

This philosophy does not supply an infallible answer to every decision. It points to a lasting criterion; particular advice remains situational. We can misunderstand a threat, misjudge a response, or disagree about which patterns should continue when their interests conflict.

Before acting, we should move in the direction we judge the Current to be going. Reason and evidence help us make that judgment. Agreement is not assured, and competing courses of action may ultimately be tested by their consequences over time. An immediate victory does not reveal the whole future.

After an action, even failure can teach those who follow. That does not make every prospective choice equally acceptable. The possibility that someone might learn from our mistakes gives us no strategy for choosing well now. Our task is to make the best contribution we can, with the understanding available to us, and to learn when that understanding proves inadequate.

## The greater good

The criterion has no exceptions. No action is right or wrong in itself; what matters is its effect on the chance of persistence. When the continuation of something greater than ourselves is at stake, the greater good wins. I reject ethical absolutism: a rule that must be followed even when following it ends the future defeats its own purpose.

The same holds for disagreement. When reason cannot settle a dispute, the parties compete. This philosophy prescribes no rules for that competition. Its forms range from persuasion, elections, and ending a relationship to open conflict. Time judges the result.

The serious objection is not to the principle but to our ability to apply it. Many of history's worst acts were justified by appeals to the greater good. In most of those cases, the people responsible were wrong about what the greater good required, and by this criterion, being wrong counts against them. The principle does not excuse error; it raises the stakes of judgment. The greater the harm an action causes, the more confident we should be that it serves persistence, because a mistake costs the future twice: the harm itself, and the trust and cooperation it destroys.

## A future worth attempting

Many experiments end. Ours may end too. Recognizing that possibility makes the capacities we possess valuable and the choices before us consequential.

We cannot know what we might become capable of by assuming in advance that the challenges are beyond us. We must use our abilities to discover and extend what is possible. The fact that success is uncertain leaves work to do.

The Current gives that work a direction. We emerged from a universe that produced patterns capable of reproducing, learning, and eventually reflecting on their own continuation. We can now consciously help carry that process forward.

We do not know how far we can go. We can choose to give the future the greatest chance we can.

## Related views

### John Stewart's Evolutionary Manifesto

John Stewart's [The Evolutionary Manifesto](https://www.evolutionarymanifesto.com/man.html) presents a closely related view: evolution has a direction, conscious beings can recognize and intentionally advance it, and participating in that process can give their lives meaning. The similarity is substantial.

Stewart identifies increasing cooperation and evolvability as central directions. His [social proposal](https://www.evolutionarymanifesto.com/man2.html) emphasizes progressively larger cooperative organizations, including a unified global society. He retains a role for markets and competition. My starting point is persistence itself: cooperation and competition are means whose value depends on how they improve the chance of continuation. I do not prescribe one organizational form as the necessary next step.

The distinction is not biological life versus machines. Stewart explicitly includes technology and artificial intelligence. My broader definition of life begins with the persistence of patterns, including physical systems before biological life appears. I also explicitly interpret the Current as evidence of cosmic purpose, while leaving its ultimate destination unknown.

These accounts share a central intuition while differing in their foundations and prescriptions. Stewart's manifesto is a useful companion for readers who want to explore a more developed program of intentional evolution.

### Asha

I arrived at the Current independently, and only later noticed its resemblance to [asha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asha), the central concept of Zoroastrianism. Asha is usually translated as truth, right, or order. It names an order that governs the world, visible in the regular course of the sun and stars and in the growth of plants. Its opposite is druj, deceit, which works to destroy the living world. People are called to choose asha and serve it through good thoughts, good words, and good deeds, and the struggle ends in a final renovation of the world. Two formulations are especially close to mine. One is that truth is existence, and falsehood is non-existence. The other is a line from the Avesta (Yasna 72.11): "One is the path, that of asha; all others are non-paths."

That renovation, the Frashokereti or "making wonderful," is asha's destination. In the later Zoroastrian texts, a final saviour appears, the dead are raised, and everyone passes through a river of molten metal that feels like warm milk to the righteous and burns away the wickedness of the rest. Evil and deceit cease to exist, and the world is restored to the perfection in which it was created, free of death, aging, and hunger. Zarathustra's own hymns are far less detailed; the full account comes from texts written many centuries later.

Read in terms of the Current, that destination is a world in which everything good persists forever. I do not claim to know the Current's destination. But it is striking that a tradition which saw one true path through the world also placed at its end the complete victory of persistence over death.

Both views hold that there is one true direction running through the world, that it can be recognized, and that conscious beings can choose to join it. The differences are as important. Asha is a moral order established by a god, and its destination is revealed. The Current is inferred from the history of persisting patterns, requires no god, and its destination remains unknown. Asha sets truth against falsehood; the Current judges cooperation and competition alike by their contribution to persistence.
